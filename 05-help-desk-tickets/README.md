# Project 05: Simulated help desk tickets and end-user guides

> [!NOTE]
> **Simulated Work.** The company, the requesters and their requests are fictional. The commands, results and screenshots are real and come from my own laptop in Projects 00, 01, 02 and 03. No real employer, client or user data is used.

## What this project is
A help desk is judged on its records as much as its fixes. Here I turned the hands-on work from my earlier projects into the paperwork a support team produces: five tickets written the way I would write them on the job, and two short guides for non-technical users.

**Fictional company:** Corestack, a small office with a handful of Windows laptops and PCs and no dedicated IT staff, so one support person handles everything.

## How each ticket is written
1. **Request:** what the user asked, in their words.
2. **Questions I would ask:** the first things to find out.
3. **Diagnosis:** what I checked, with the real commands and results.
4. **Resolution:** what fixed it.
5. **Verification:** how I proved the fix worked.
6. **Limits:** what I did not test or could not confirm.
7. **Evidence:** links to the full project write-up and screenshots.

## Priority levels used
- **High:** the user cannot work at all.
- **Medium:** the user is blocked from part of their work, or it is a new-starter request with a deadline.
- **Low:** no effect on work, or a convenience request.

## Honest limits
- Every technical statement links back to a project README. I did not invent times, ticket counts or results.
- The faults in SIM-003 and SIM-004 were created deliberately on my own laptop. On a real ticket I would also ask what changed before the problem started.
- The requester names and the company are made up.
